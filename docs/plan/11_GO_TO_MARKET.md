# 11 — Go-to-Market (Marketing Lead hat)

**TL;DR**
1. The first 100 users come from named pre-arrival communities, reached by the founder in person, in the six weeks before an arrival wave.
2. The message is one line: "Know what to do next." The proof is the housing cost check, which is useful in 60 seconds without a pitch.
3. Organic loops come before any spend: community answers, city guides, invite links. Paid acquisition is not used before R3 data exists.
4. The landing page, store listings and city guides all obey the product's safety rules: no invented prices, places or claims.
5. A weekly 30-minute review decides what to repeat and what to stop.

---

## 1. Ideal customer profile

| Attribute | First 100 users |
|---|---|
| Who | International master's and exchange students, and domestic first-time renters, aged 18–26 |
| When | Admitted or committed; arrival in 1–8 weeks; housing not yet secured is the strongest signal |
| Where (launch assumption A-01) | Moving to large US university cities; English-speaking; Android and iPhone |
| Trigger moments | Visa approved or flight booked; a listing found in a group chat; "what do I do first?" posts; two weeks before move-in |
| Not yet | Families, professionals with relocation packages, anyone needing visa or academic help |

Arrival waves (A-13): January (spring intake) and August–September (autumn intake). Outreach windows: mid-November to mid-January, and May to mid-August.

## 2. The first 100 users

Assumptions are stated so the plan can be falsified. Conversion numbers are planning estimates, not measurements.

| # | Channel | Tactic | Volume assumption | Expected onboarded users |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-departure group chats (WhatsApp, Telegram, Discord) of incoming students at 5 target universities, usually run by student associations | Ask an admin for permission; post one useful thing (the move-in cash checklist) with the link; answer questions for a week | 5 groups × 300 members; 8% click; 40% onboard | ~48 |
| 2 | Reddit communities for international students and for specific universities and cities | Answer real questions ("how much cash do I need for move-in?") with a worked example and a disclosure line; no link-dropping; follow each community's self-promotion rule | 20 helpful answers; 150 views each; 2% click; 35% onboard | ~21 |
| 3 | Facebook housing and "incoming class" groups | Post the scam checklist as an image-free text post; offer to run anyone's listing through the cost check in comments | 6 groups; 40 engaged people; 30% onboard | ~12 |
| 4 | Personal network and warm introductions (friends who studied abroad, former classmates, student ambassadors) | Ten 20-minute calls; each person shares with three movers | 30 introductions; 50% onboard | ~15 |
| 5 | Student associations at the 5 universities (international student associations, cultural societies) | Offer a free 20-minute "first week" session for their newcomers; the product is the handout | 2 sessions × 25 attendees; 40% onboard | ~20 |
| | **Total** | | | **~116** |

Names and posting rules of specific communities must be checked before use (V-41). Target cities for the first cohort: pick five where the founder has at least one contact; record them on the board.

**Scripts**

*Group admin request:* "Hi — I'm building a free tool that helps incoming students work out what to do before and after they land (real monthly housing cost, move-in cash, first-week checklist). No ads, nothing for sale. Could I share it once in the group? Happy to answer questions there."

*Useful post:* "Move-in cash is usually more than first month's rent: deposit, application fee, SIM, bedding, first groceries, transit card. If you paste your numbers here [link] it adds them up against your budget and flags common scam signs. It's free and in beta; I built it and I'm here for feedback."

*Reply with disclosure:* "Worked example for your numbers: rent 780 + utilities 85 + transit 64 + laundry 20 = 949 a month; move-in ≈ 780 + 780 deposit + 50 fee + ~310 setup = ~1,920. (Disclosure: I make a free tool that does this sum — link in my profile.)"

Rules: one post per community per wave; reply to every comment within a day; never post in a community that bans it; never message minors or post in school-age communities.

## 3. Positioning and messaging hierarchy

| Level | Message |
|---|---|
| One line | **Know what to do next.** |
| Promise | Tell us where you're going, when you arrive and your budget. We'll show you what matters first. |
| Pillar 1 — Clarity | A short, ordered list for before arrival, your first 24 hours and your first week. |
| Pillar 2 — Honest money | The real monthly cost and move-in cash for any listing, checked against your budget. |
| Pillar 3 — Safety | Scam warning signs, a late-arrival plan, and nothing invented. |
| Pillar 4 — Yours | Free, private, no ads, works offline on arrival day. |
| Proof | Screens of a real plan; the cost breakdown; "we never invent listings or prices". |
| What we never say | "Guaranteed", "verified listings", "find you an apartment", "AI knows best", anything about visas or admissions. |

## 5. SEO and content seeds (R3)

**Programmatic wedge:** "First week in <city>" guides for newcomers (S-R3-008 to S-R3-010).

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Template sections | Getting from the airport; phone and data on day one; where newcomers usually shop for basics (categories, not rankings); transit basics; emergency and non-emergency numbers; a first-week checklist; "run your housing numbers" call to action |
| Data source | A reviewed JSON file per city in `apps/site/src/content/cities/`, validated by a Zod schema: every factual field has `sourceUrl` and `verifiedOn`; the build fails if either is missing or older than 12 months |
| Never | Prices that are not sourced and dated; named businesses as recommendations; safety or crime claims; rent estimates; anything generated and published without human review |
| First five | The five cohort cities from section 2 |
| Scale rule | Add a city only when a person has verified its page; no bulk generation |
| Other seeds | "How much cash do I need to move in?" (calculator explainer); "Seven warning signs in a rental listing"; "Your first 24 hours in a new city" |
| Measurement | Search impressions and clicks per guide; guide → app start rate |

## 8. Referral loop (S-R3-012, S-R3-013)

Moment: after a "Compatible" verdict is saved, after the first-week tasks are done, and in Settings. Mechanic: a personal link `…/i/<code>`; the invitee lands on sign-in with "Invited by a friend who is moving too". Reward: none monetary; both see a small "moving together" line. Measured by `referral_link_shared` → `referral_signup_completed`. Target: 0.3 invites sent per activated user, 25% acceptance.

## 9. Community strategy and templates

- Be a member first: answer housing-cost and first-week questions with worked examples, with or without mentioning the product.
- Keep a public changelog paragraph in each community that allowed a post; close the loop on feedback ("you asked for miles — it's in").
- Collect permissioned quotes only after someone volunteers one.
- Office hour: one open 30-minute call a week during arrival waves.

*Status note template (used by the traffic runbook):* "We're getting more visitors than expected. Sign-ups are paused for a few hours; if you already have a plan it is safe and still works. We'll post here when the door is open again."

*Feedback ask:* "You used the housing check last week — what was missing or confusing? One sentence is plenty."

## 12. Metrics and the weekly review

Thirty minutes, same day each week, notes appended to `docs/runbooks/weekly-review.md`.

1. North star: Weekly Acting Movers, versus last week.
2. Funnel: sign-ins → onboarding completed → activated; the worst step.
3. Retention: D7 for the cohort that onboarded two weeks ago.
4. Sources: onboarded users by channel (self-reported "How did you hear about us?" in R2; referral codes in R3).
5. AI: fallback rate, helpful rate, cost per active user.
6. Top three user quotes or complaints.
7. One decision: repeat, change or stop one thing. Write it on the board.

## 13. Ethics checklist for marketing to young and international users

- [ ] Every claim on a page or post is true today and can be shown in the product.
- [ ] No urgency tricks, countdown offers or fear-based headlines about scams.
- [ ] Disclosure in every post that the author built the product.
- [ ] No outreach to anyone known to be under 16; no school-age communities.
- [ ] No promises about housing availability, prices, visas, safety of an area or academic outcomes.
- [ ] Partner relationships are labelled where they appear; partners never change guidance.
- [ ] No personal data from communities is collected or reused; quotes only with written permission.
- [ ] Translations of marketing copy are reviewed by a fluent speaker before publication.
- [ ] Unsubscribe and "delete my data" work before any email is sent.
- [ ] Community rules are read and followed; a removed post is not reposted.
